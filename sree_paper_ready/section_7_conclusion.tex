We have presented a mechanistic probing study of variable role representations
and syntactic structure in Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B across multiple
programming languages. Linear probes on the residual stream reliably decode all
five variable roles---accumulator, index, iterator, boolean, and class
reference---with macro-F1 of $0.85$--$0.99$: iterator representations stabilize
at F1~$\approx 0.93$--$0.94$ by layers~8--9, boolean usage context is decodable
at $\approx 0.9$ across Python, Java, and Go, and class references reach
$\approx 0.93$--$0.95$ by layer~3--4 with positive selectivity ($0.15$--$0.20$)
confirming signal beyond token identity. The most striking role-level finding is
a dissociation in cue type: index detection degrades sharply under misleading
names ($\Delta\text{F1} = {-}0.272$ for Qwen2.5-1.5B, $-0.285$ for
DeepSeek-Coder-1.3B) while accumulator detection \emph{improves}
($+0.079$ F1), revealing qualitatively different mechanisms for different roles.
At the syntactic level, coarse token class is near-perfectly decodable by
layer~2 (macro-F1~$\approx 0.97$--$0.99$), though a Hewitt control shows most
of this is token-identity memorization (selectivity only $0.20$--$0.27$); code
pretraining shifts the resolution layer earlier (L8~$\rightarrow$~L3) without
raising the ceiling, and identifier-role cross-lingual transfer degrades to
$0.58$--$0.65$ in Coder's late layers. Finally, PCA of last-token activations
shows that language identity is geometrically structured from layer~0 onwards,
peaking at layer~4 ($r = {+}0.941$, PC1 accounting for $37\%$ of variance),
with 3D projections revealing tight within-language manifolds and interpretable
sub-groupings such as C/C++ and Java/C\# pairs. All probing results are
correlational; future work should establish causal relevance via activation
patching and sparse autoencoder analysis, and extend coverage to larger models
and the remaining accumulator cross-language conditions (JavaScript and PHP).
